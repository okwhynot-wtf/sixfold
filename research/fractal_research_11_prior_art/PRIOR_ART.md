# Published relatives of the six-lobe fractal

17 November 2026

The closest geometric relative I found is the Sierpiński octahedron described by Aimee Kunnen and Steven Schlicker. The closest programmable relative is Syntopia’s octahedral folding construction, based on Knighty’s work. Both precede this project. The literature also contains connected three-dimensional fractals that fail the usual separation conditions. Across these sources I found no description of our particular one-parameter family or its combination of periodic contact, exact dimensions at two members, and independently detectable tunnels. Historical priority remains open.

## The closest geometric relatives

**The regular Sierpiński octahedron.** Kunnen and Schlicker studied vertex-based copies of regular polyhedra in 1998. Their octahedron uses six copies at half scale. At our reference height the six translation vectors also point to the vertices of a regular octahedron. The difference is easiest to see at the centre. Write the classical maps as \(f_v(x)=(x+v)/2\), with \(v\) one of the six vertices. The opposite vertex \(-v\) belongs to the attractor, so \(f_v(-v)=0\) for every \(v\). All six pieces contain the centre. Our central axis has positive clearance. Each lobe touches four others and stays a positive distance from its opposite. The classical Hausdorff dimension is \(\log 6/\log 2=2.58496\ldots\), above the upper bound of 2.415 for every member of our certified family. This also rules out any equivalence that distorts all distances by at most fixed factors, called a bi-Lipschitz equivalence. [Kunnen and Schlicker, *Regular Sierpinski Polyhedra*, pp. 607–619](https://pme-math.org/journal/issues/PMEJ.Vol.10.No.8.pdf) · [Project contact classification](../fractal_handover/HANDOVER.md) · [Central passage and dimensions](../fractal_research_08/RESEARCH_08.md)

**Rotated octahedra in mathematical art.** Syntopia’s Fragmentarium source credits Knighty and labels its octahedron distance estimator 2010. The version linked here was committed in July 2011. It rotates the point, folds space using signed coordinate swaps, scales and offsets the result, then rotates again. It includes named examples such as *Rose* and *Skeleton*. This is a documented predecessor for the artistic idea of twisting an octahedral fractal. The rotation calculation below distinguishes the actual one-step rules at every choice of the shader’s settings. There is also a 2022 artwork explicitly titled *Sierpinski’s octahedron with a twist*. Its creator describes the rendering technique. I could not identify that animation’s exact branch maps from the creator’s posts, so its mathematical relationship remains unresolved. [Fragmentarium source and presets](https://github.com/Syntopia/Fragmentarium/blob/14130ad5dcf8024868e7e2d34c64d911329fd83c/Fragmentarium-Source/Examples/Kaleidoscopic%20IFS/Octahedron.frag) · [Artist’s 2022 post and discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/fractals/comments/slg2ps/)

**Other spatial examples.** Hideki Tsuiki’s triangular-antiprismoid fractal is an especially relevant comparison by shape. Its six-vertex polyhedron is an octahedron, and the fractal has dimension two and square shadows in selected directions. Robert Fathauer’s *Fractal Crystal* builds a regular-octahedral outline with repeated triangular holes by putting smaller cubes on exposed faces. The classical Menger sponge is the natural tunnel comparison. Its twenty one-third-scale pieces give Hausdorff dimension \(\log 20/\log 3=2.72683\ldots\). That distinguishes its metric geometry from every member of our curve. Whether the spaces might be homeomorphic, allowing arbitrary continuous deformation with continuous inverse, is still an open project question. [Tsuiki, Bridges 2007](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2007/bridges2007-277.html) · [Fathauer, Bridges 2008](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2008/bridges2008-289.html) · [Baez on the Menger sponge](https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/visual_insight/20140301%20-%20Menger%20Sponge.html)

## A rotation test for the closest published shader

This comparison follows from the published code. On any one linear branch of a single fold iteration, its orthogonal part has the form \(A P_i B\). The rotations \(A,B\) are common to all branches. Each \(P_i\) is a signed permutation matrix because every fold exchanges coordinates and possibly changes their signs. For any two branches, the relative orthogonal matrix is therefore conjugate to a signed permutation. The same holds when taking the inverse branches to compare contracting maps. The possible traces of a three-dimensional orientation-preserving signed permutation are \(-1,0,1,3\). Equivalently its possible principal rotation angles are \(180°,120°,90°,0°\). [Fragmentarium fold implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Syntopia/Fragmentarium/14130ad5dcf8024868e7e2d34c64d911329fd83c/Fragmentarium-Source/Examples/Kaleidoscopic%20IFS/Octahedron.frag)

For our first two lobes, orient the axes so both turns have positive angle \(\alpha\). The axes then have dot product \(-1/2\). With \(u=\tan(\alpha/2)\), quaternion multiplication gives the scalar part of \(Q_0^{-1}Q_1\) as \(s=(1-u^2/2)/(1+u^2)\), hence its trace is \(4s^2-1\). The certified family lies inside \(0.2675<u<0.2678\). Substituting these rational endpoints bounds the trace strictly between 2.23 and 2.24 throughout. The reference relative angle is about **51.75048°**. A simultaneous change of coordinates or a relabelling of the branches preserves the collection of relative traces. Thus no settings of this shader reproduce our six maps as its literal one-step inverse branches up to a global similarity. Equality of limit sets built by different maps, or by grouping multiple steps, is a separate question. [Project rotation matrices and parameter bounds](../fractal_handover/fractal_research_07/RESEARCH_07.md)

## Published precedents for the overlap and the holes

- **Connectedness in space despite failure of separation.** Kamalutdinov, Tetenov and Vaulin constructed a self-similar Jordan arc in three dimensions whose pieces meet at most at single points and whose defining system fails both the open set condition and weak separation. Their public 2015 preprint is a close precedent for the overlap behaviour. A Jordan arc is homeomorphic to an interval. Our loops have nonzero winding detected in the full attractor, so this example can be ruled out even as a topological match. Kamalutdinov and Tetenov also published a six-map example on the line in which one contact coexists with failure of the open set condition and equality of Hausdorff and similarity dimensions. That set is totally disconnected. [Spatial arc, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.00290) · [Six-map example, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08595) · [Project winding certificates](../fractal_research_10_topology/RESEARCH_10.md)
- **Holes in an overlapping family.** Broomhead, Montaldi and Sidorov’s *Golden gaskets* vary the common scale of three planar copies. Their family has holes amid overlaps and exactly computable dimensions at distinguished parameters. At the multinacci parameters different finite compositions coincide as maps. In our reference example two finite compositions share a fixed point while all distinct finite words give distinct maps. These are genuinely different ways for two symbolic descriptions to reach the same place. Bandt and Mekhontsev also published connected planar carpets with irrational rotations, found by computer-assisted search, under the open set condition. [Golden gaskets](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309304) · [Bandt and Mekhontsev](https://doi.org/10.3390/fractalfract6010039) · [Project proof distinguishing finite maps](../fractal_research_08/RESEARCH_08.md)
- **The dimension gap and shrinking loops.** Fraser, Henderson, Olson and Robinson studied the Hausdorff–Assouad dimension gap for overlapping self-similar sets. García’s Corollary 1.6 gives full Assouad dimension under the rotation and overlap hypotheses verified in this project. Hochman’s Corollary 1.7 supplies the algebraic dimension theorem used at our two selected parameters. Shrinking loops that encode independent integer windings have a classical model in the Hawaiian earring. The project-specific work is proving the hypotheses and showing that the curves detecting our windings avoid *every* overlapping copy of the spatial set. [Fraser and coauthors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.1016) · [García](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.03148) · [Hochman](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.09043) · [Hatcher, Example 1.25](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT-doublepage.pdf)

## What appears to be new

The strongest candidate is the family selected by an exact geometric condition. Six equal-scale copies alternate height and tilt about tangent axes. The scale and tilt are chosen so that repeating either of two eight-step itineraries, 01153513 and 10024204, reaches the same point on a half-turn axis. The two motions about that point have different axes. The contact persists along the certified height interval \([0.7070,0.7072]\). Throughout that interval the construction gives a connected set with independent loops at arbitrarily small scales, Hausdorff dimension below 2.415, and Assouad dimension 3. The last dimension measures the most demanding local covering behaviour. [Contact equations](../fractal_handover/HANDOVER.md) · [Dimension deduction](../fractal_handover/fractal_research_07/RESEARCH_07.md) · [Loops in the full attractor](../fractal_research_10_topology/RESEARCH_10.md)

There is also evidence for a family in a stronger sense than a continuously changing drawing. At \(h=1/\sqrt2\) the project obtains Hausdorff dimension \(2.4148914586\ldots\). At the member with \(u=19/71\) it obtains \(2.4149052346\ldots\). The difference separates at least two members up to bi-Lipschitz equivalence. Pairwise inequivalence of the whole curve remains open. These are the project’s computer-assisted mathematical results, pending independent review. [Two exact dimensions and their derivations](../fractal_research_08/RESEARCH_08.md)

The search covered journal papers, arXiv, cited predecessors, the Bridges mathematical-art archive, publicly shared artwork and original renderer source. Search terms included *Sierpiński octahedron*, *polyhedral gasket*, *triangular antiprismoid*, *rotated octahedron*, *kaleidoscopic IFS*, *periodic contact*, *self-similar Jordan arc*, and *weak separation*. Informal presets with incomplete formulas and equivalent attractors described by quite different maps are the main remaining uncertainties.

For a first paper I would lead with the explicit family and its two dimension calculations. Include the one-step rotation test for the closest documented renderer and a single figure marking the shared periodic point and a linked loop. The next mathematical target is to distinguish more members by an intrinsic invariant such as Hausdorff dimension. An independent check of the contact and dimension arguments would make the family a stronger result regardless of whether an older rendering eventually turns up.
